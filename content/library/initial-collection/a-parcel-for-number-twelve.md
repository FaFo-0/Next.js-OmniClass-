# A Parcel for Number Twelve

The delivery app said "Handed to resident", but there was no parcel outside Mariam's door. Inside it were the shoes she needed for a job interview the following morning. She had ordered them early enough, or so she thought.

She checked the photograph attached to the message. It showed a brown box beside a green door. Her own door was white.

Mariam had moved into the building only a week before. She didn't want her first conversation with a neighbour to sound like an accusation. She walked along the corridor, looking for the door in the picture.

Number twelve was green. A man answered after her second knock.

"Sorry to bother you," she began. "I think the driver may have left my parcel here. There's a photo, but I can't see the number clearly."

The man looked behind him. "A box arrived this afternoon. My daughter orders things, so I thought it was hers. What's the name?"

Mariam told him. He picked up the box and checked the label.

"That's you. I should have looked more carefully."

She felt herself relax. "The driver probably saw twelve instead of twenty-one."

As he handed it over, he noticed the shoe shop's name. "Interview?" he asked.

"Tomorrow. How did you guess?"

"My daughter bought a pair from there before her first office job. Good luck."

Back in her flat, Mariam tried on the shoes. They fitted. She also corrected the delivery instructions in the app: third floor, white door, number twenty-one.

The next evening, she met the man by the lift. He asked how the interview had gone. She hadn't heard the result yet, but she told him about the friendly manager and the questions she had managed to answer.

The parcel problem had lasted less than an hour. Knowing someone in the building lasted longer.
