# The Cheapest Room in the City

The room looked almost suspiciously affordable. It had a desk, a private bathroom and a window overlooking a quiet courtyard. For Karim, who was attending a two-day training course, it seemed ideal. The listing said "twenty minutes from the centre". He booked before the last discounted room disappeared.

Only after the confirmation arrived did he check the address properly. Twenty minutes meant twenty minutes by car in light traffic. By public transport, the journey involved two buses and a long walk. His course began at eight thirty, and the first bus wouldn't get him there in time.

He contacted the host. His first draft sounded angry: "Your description is misleading. I want a refund." Before sending it, he read the cancellation conditions again. The room was non-refundable, and the description hadn't actually claimed that the journey was possible by bus.

He rewrote the message. He explained his course times, asked whether there was an earlier transport option, and said that the location would make his stay difficult. Could the host offer a partial refund if the room was booked by someone else?

The host replied that evening. There was a shared taxi used by local workers, but its route changed according to demand. She couldn't promise when it would arrive. However, she was willing to release the room for another booking. If it sold, she would refund the room charge, keeping the booking fee.

Karim had to decide whether to risk paying for two rooms. He calculated the likely taxi fares, the time spent travelling and the cost of a hotel within walking distance of the course. The central hotel was more expensive per night, but the total difference was smaller than he had assumed.

He chose it. The original room was eventually booked for one of the two nights, so he recovered part of the cost. It wasn't the bargain he had hoped for, but it was manageable.

At the course, another participant praised the same cheap listing. Karim didn't tell her to avoid it. She was travelling by car and visiting family nearby; for her, the location made sense.

He showed her the map instead. "Ask what the travel time includes," he said. "The room may be cheap. Getting to the place you need can be the expensive part."
