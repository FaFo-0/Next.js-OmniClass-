# One Afternoon without a Map

Sofia's phone switched off just as she left the museum. She pressed the power button twice, although she knew the battery was empty. Her charger was in her hotel room. So were the paper map and the card with the hotel's address.

She remembered that the hotel was near a river, opposite a bakery. Unfortunately, she had passed several bakeries that morning.

For a moment, she considered taking a taxi. Then she realised she couldn't tell the driver where to go. She needed information before she needed transport.

Inside a bookshop, she asked whether she could charge her phone. The assistant didn't have the right cable, but he offered her a street map.

"Do you remember the name of the hotel?"

"The Riverside House. A small place with a blue sign."

He found it in a local guide and marked it on the map. It was farther away than she expected.

"You can walk along the river," he said. "It takes about forty minutes. Or take a bus from the square."

Sofia wasn't in a hurry. She bought a postcard, thanked him and chose the walk. At the square, she asked a woman which road led to the river. This time, she repeated the directions back: "Past the post office, then left at the bridge?"

"Exactly. Don't cross the bridge. Stay on this side."

Without a screen to follow, Sofia noticed details she had missed earlier: painted doors, a tiny garden between two buildings, and people carrying warm bread home. She stopped to draw the garden on the back of her postcard.

At the hotel, she plugged in her phone and wrote the address on a small piece of paper for her bag. She still wanted digital maps. She also wanted the confidence to manage when one wasn't available.
